## Bottom line

Sh44.6 billion collected from SGR passenger fares and freight charges is sitting idle in accounts at KCB, and Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi has said restrictions under the financing agreement keep Kenya from using it. The reported cause is the 2014 Exim loan, which uses a joint account as security. That is a contractual limit on how the state handles its own revenue, and the public has not seen the clause. Separately, the headline that Kenya "saves Sh22 billion" and the Treasury claim of about Sh27 billion in annual savings are different measurements. They should not be added or swapped.

## The parties and the instrument

The instrument is the 2014 loan from the Export Import Bank of China that financed the Standard Gauge Railway. The reported mechanism is a joint account held as security for the loan, with a minimum balance of about Sh25 billion. The sums held now exceed that floor by a wide margin, yet the Treasury says they cannot be freely used. Reuters reported in August 2025 that Kenya was in talks to convert the loan to yuan, which makes the account arrangement part of a live negotiation and not only a historical term.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 201 of the Constitution commits public finance to openness and accountability. Article 211 gives Parliament the power to set the terms on which the national government borrows. Article 35 gives citizens a right of access to information held by the state. The question for each is the same: whether terms that restrict public revenue were disclosed to the body that authorises borrowing. This brief does not answer that, because the signed text and the record of parliamentary approval are not in hand.

### Two savings figures, two measures

The Sh21.61 billion reduction compares what Kenya paid in the year to June 2026 with what it paid before. The Sh27 billion figure is described as annual savings and is attached to a dollar amount, which suggests a currency or restructuring effect. Without the Treasury's workings the two cannot be reconciled, and a reader should not assume they describe the same saving.

### Revenue the state cannot touch

A joint account used as loan security is a common lender protection. What is unusual is the scale: the balance is far above the reported minimum, and the cost of idle money falls on a railway and a Treasury that both report financial strain. The disclosure gap, not the existence of security, is the finding.

## The power reading

The lender's position is ordinary. It holds security over the revenue of the asset it financed. The weaker party is not Kenya as a state but Kenya as a public that was never shown the term. The yuan conversion talks matter because they are the moment when the account arrangement can be renegotiated.

## What to watch

- Publication or tabling of the 2014 Exim loan agreement and its account terms.
- The outcome of the yuan conversion talks reported in August 2025.
- A Treasury reconciliation of the Sh21.61 billion and Sh27 billion savings figures.
- Any release of the idle balance or a change to the minimum.
