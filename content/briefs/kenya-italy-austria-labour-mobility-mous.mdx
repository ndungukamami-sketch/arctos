## Bottom line

Kenya signed a Migration and Mobility memorandum with Italy on 2 July 2026 in Nairobi, and a labour and migration memorandum with Austria that Mudavadi welcomed in mid July. The Italian memorandum sets a framework for regular labour migration, skills development and worker protection, and commits both governments to cooperate on returning nationals in irregular situations. A separate Letter of Intent on security and crime was signed alongside it. No quotas, sectors, recruitment fee rules or binding status have been reported for either deal.

## The parties and the instruments

Italy: Prime Cabinet Secretary Musalia Mudavadi and Interior Minister Matteo Piantedosi signed the memorandum. It covers vocational and language training, legal migration pathways and awareness of the risks of irregular migration, and favours voluntary return where possible, under each country's national law.

Austria: the memorandum is described as a pathway for skilled Kenyans. Healthcare is the main sector named, with Kenyan health professionals hired by KABEG Hospital in Carinthia. A separate Framework Agreement for Financial Cooperation provides about €40 million in concessional financing. The reporting does not describe worker protections.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

A memorandum of understanding is usually not a treaty, so it may not reach Parliament under the Treaty Making and Ratification Act, 2012. That leaves the protections for workers dependent on the text and on later regulation of recruitment agencies. Headline reports of two more deals, with Norway for seafarers and Qatar, were not verified.

## What the record shows

- The Italian memorandum pairs labour pathways with cooperation on return of irregular migrants.
- The Italian security letter was signed on the same occasion, and its terms are not reported.
- The Austrian financing line is separate from the labour memorandum, and the sources do not say they are linked.

## The power reading

European states seek labour and managed migration, while Kenya seeks jobs and remittances. The balance sits in the return commitments and in who regulates recruitment fees. Both are unspecified.

## What to watch

- Publication of either text, and whether it states quotas, sectors or fee limits.
- The content of the Italian Letter of Intent on security and crime.
- Whether Parliament is asked to review either instrument.
