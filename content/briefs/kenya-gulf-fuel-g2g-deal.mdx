## Bottom line

Kenya imports nearly all its fuel under government to government arrangements with Gulf suppliers, which began in 2023 with Aramco Trading Fujairah, ADNOC Global Trading and ENOC. The Gulf crisis that began in February 2026 has tested them. Pump prices rose in April and May, inflation climbed, and transport operators struck before the government pledged a diesel price cut. Energy Minister Opiyo Wandayi says the framework has stabilised supply, fixed freight and insurance costs and allows more cargoes from Europe, the US Gulf coast, India and the Red Sea. Opposition critics call it opaque and say it has not secured competitive prices. The contracts have not been published, so neither side can be checked.

## The parties and the instrument

The instruments are supply arrangements between the Government of Kenya and the Gulf national oil companies and traders, with Saudi Aramco and ADNOC named as major suppliers. The public record does not give volumes, credit period or premium. The minister cites fixed freight and insurance costs but gives no figures.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 227 of the Constitution requires public procurement to be fair, transparent and cost effective, and Article 35 gives a right of access to information. Whether a government to government arrangement that bypasses open tendering meets those tests is the long running dispute. This brief makes no finding on it.

## The power reading

A government that buys through a handful of state linked suppliers gains continuity and loses price competition. When a crisis hits the supplier region, the same concentration becomes a risk. The balance depends on terms that remain private.

## What to watch

- Publication of volumes, credit terms and premiums, or a parliamentary or Auditor General review.
- The share of cargoes sourced outside the Gulf.
- Whether the arrangement is renewed or restructured after the crisis.
