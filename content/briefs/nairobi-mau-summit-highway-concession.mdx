## Bottom line

The original concession was valued at $1.93 billion (Ksh249.7 billion). The renegotiated one is $1.342 billion (Ksh173.5 billion), a reported saving of Ksh76.2 billion. The structure also changes, from about Ksh23 billion a year in payments by the state to a toll model paid by road users. The saving is real on the headline, but the burden moves to motorists, and the payment of Sh6.2 billion to the French consortium without MPs' approval raises a separate question about authority to spend.

## The parties and the instrument

The counterparty is a French led consortium holding the concession to upgrade and operate the Nairobi to Mau Summit corridor. The instrument is a concession agreement, renegotiated after the original terms drew objection. The Sh6.2 billion payment is reported to have been made before parliamentary approval.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Public money may be spent only with parliamentary authority, and Article 201 sets openness and accountability as principles of public finance. Which provision governs the Sh6.2 billion payment depends on facts this brief does not have, and it makes no finding on legality.

## The power reading

Renegotiation shows Kenya had leverage once the terms were challenged. The risk that remains is the one tolling creates: traffic and pricing assumptions decide who gains, and they are not public.

## What to watch

- Publication of the renegotiated concession and its toll schedule.
- Parliamentary review of the Sh6.2 billion payment.
- Traffic projections and the duration of the concession.
