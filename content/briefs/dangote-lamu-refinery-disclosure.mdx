## Bottom line

Aliko Dangote and President Ruto broke ground in late September 2026 on a planned $16 billion refinery in Lamu, with a stated capacity of 700,000 barrels a day and about 40 months to completion. Reports say Kenya has considered taking 10 percent, about $500 million, of a combined 30 percent Dangote has offered East African governments, while Ruto says the government will take its stake through the National Infrastructure Fund using public assets including land. Ruto has defended keeping the agreement private and told critics to seek it through Parliament. Politicians and a consumer group demand the full agreement, the shareholders and the source of the money, and two courts are involved.

## The parties and the instrument

The counterparties are the Government of Kenya and a Dangote group company. The instrument is an agreement not made public, with undisclosed side letters alleged by critics and unknown beneficial owners per a request by Senator Edwin Sifuna. The government has identified about 9,000 acres and seeks another 3,000 for a special economic zone and new city. A Kenya News Agency report of 6 October also covers Fred Matiang'i's demand for disclosure of mining concession deals, which this brief does not examine.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 35 of the Constitution gives citizens a right of access to information held by the state. Matiang'i argues that commercial or security concerns should not shield contracts from Parliament. Public land and public funds committed to a private project engage Articles 201 and 227, but the sources do not show how either was applied.

## What the record shows

- High Court, Nairobi: a petition by Francis Awino was certified urgent on 6 October, with a next mention on 12 November.
- Environment and Land Court, Malindi: 133 residents challenge a parcel in Hindi and Manda Magogoni as ancestral land, and the court ordered the status quo, with a return date of 14 October.
- Dangote's Lagos refinery figures are separate and are sometimes mixed up with the Lamu ones.

## The power reading

A buyer that is also a landowner and a shareholder shifts risk to the state. The undisclosed agreement leaves Kenya's exposure on land, incentives and guarantees unmeasured, which is what the petitions try to bring out.

## What to watch

- The 12 November High Court mention and any order for disclosure.
- The outcome of the Malindi land case.
- Whether Parliament or the Treasury confirms the stake size, price and funding route.
