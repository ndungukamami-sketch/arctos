## Bottom line

Bloomberg reported on 11 June 2026 that Kenya would pay China Communications Construction Company (CCCC) $2.9 billion, about Sh375 billion, to upgrade Jomo Kenyatta International Airport. On 17 June Treasury Cabinet Secretary John Mbadi said the figure he knows is $1.2 billion, about Sh155.34 billion. Both cannot describe the same obligation. Until the contract is published, the public cannot tell whether the gap is scope, phasing, financing or a plain disagreement. A 12 October press item repeating the $2.9 billion figure is a re-report, not new evidence.

## The parties and the instrument

The counterparties are the Government of Kenya and CCCC. The instrument is reported as an upgrade contract, and its structure is not confirmed. Financing is unclear: reporting points variously to direct government funding, loans backed by the passenger service charge, and the National Infrastructure Fund. This matters because the replaced deal was a different kind of instrument. The Adani proposal, cancelled in November 2024, was a 30 year concession valued at $1.85 billion, which is a lease of the asset, while this appears to be a construction contract paid for by the state.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 227 of the Constitution requires public procurement to be fair, equitable, transparent, competitive and cost effective. Article 35 provides for access to information held by the state. Whether either was met depends on how CCCC was selected and what was published, neither of which is confirmed in the sources found. This brief does not make a finding on procurement.

### The number that two officials do not share

A difference between $2.9 billion and $1.2 billion is larger than any rounding or exchange rate effect. Plausible explanations are phasing, scope or what is counted, but none is documented. The honest reading is that the public record contains two figures and no instrument.

## The power reading

A state that funds the build carries the cost and the risk, and a contractor that is also a lender, if that is the structure, gains leverage over variations. None of that can be confirmed. The Adani cancellation shows Kenya will reverse a deal when public pressure builds, which is why publication of the terms matters now.

## What to watch

- Publication of the award, the contract and the financing agreement.
- A Treasury statement reconciling the $2.9 billion and $1.2 billion figures.
- Any parliamentary scrutiny or court challenge to the selection of CCCC.
