## Bottom line

DP World and the Kenyan firm GulfCap Africa signed a shareholders agreement on 9 September 2026 in Nairobi to develop the Mombasa Industrial Park, a planned 222 hectare special economic zone. Phase one covers 40 hectares. More than 20,000 direct and indirect jobs are expected and more than 60 companies have expressed interest. President Ruto described the deal as a tripartite agreement between DP World, the County Government of Mombasa and GulfCap, and said the government will provide the infrastructure. The investment amount and the land arrangement are not stated. The partnership was first announced in early August, and one Radar headline garbled the names as "DB World and Gulf Cup Africa".

## The parties and the instrument

The instrument is a shareholders agreement for a joint venture. The Radar rationale called it a land concession, which the sources do not support: no land transfer, lease or concession terms are described. The public role is the county's participation and the national government's commitment to infrastructure.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 10 of the Constitution sets transparency and public participation among the national values. The questions here are practical: what land the county contributes, on what terms, and what public money the promised infrastructure will cost. The sources do not describe any of the three.

## What the record shows

- Signed 9 September 2026 in Nairobi, after an announcement in early August.
- 222 hectares, with phase one at 40 hectares.
- Market access is pitched through AfCFTA, the EU Economic Partnership Agreement and the UAE CEPA.
- Investment amount and county stake: not stated.

## The power reading

DP World brings capital and a logistics network, while the county and the national government bring land and infrastructure. Whoever contributes the land at a favourable price carries the public risk. Publication of the shareholding and land terms would settle it.

## What to watch

- The shareholding split and the land contribution by Mombasa County.
- The cost of the infrastructure the national government has promised.
- Whether any tax or customs incentives are attached to the zone.
