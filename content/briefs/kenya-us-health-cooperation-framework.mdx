## Bottom line

Kenya and the United States have a five year health cooperation framework valued at $1.6 billion, about Sh206.9 billion, with Kenya adding about $850 million, or roughly Sh110 billion. The framework says Kenya will not share sensitive personal data and will share only aggregated data. Critics point to immunity clauses for US personnel and contractors and to the volume of HIV and TB data in the programmes. The High Court halted implementation in December 2025. The Court of Appeal later lifted that order on an interim basis, and a ruling on the underlying case was reported as scheduled for 30 October. The 15 July 2026 signing reported by the press was the implementing agreement of a framework signed on 4 December 2025 by Marco Rubio and Musalia Mudavadi.

## The parties and the instrument

The instruments are a bilateral framework, a Data Sharing Agreement and an implementing agreement between the two governments. The Treasury Principal Secretary announced on 8 June 2026 that the governments had agreed on implementation. The US paused its own rollout in February 2026 to respect the court process.

## The constitutional and treaty backdrop

Article 2(6) of the Constitution makes a treaty or convention ratified by Kenya part of Kenyan law. Senator Okiya Omtatah asks the court to decide whether the framework is a treaty or international agreement under that article, and whether mandatory parliamentary approval was obtained. He also raises public participation, fiscal governance and sovereignty. The Consumer Federation of Kenya warns that misused medical data cannot be recalled by Kenyan courts or regulators. The Attorney General says the Data Protection Act governs personal data transfers, not aggregate data.

## The power reading

The framework gives Kenya a large, predictable health financing stream at a time of uncertainty over US aid, and gives the US structured access to programme data. The risk is concentrated in two clauses: how aggregated data is defined, and the scope of the immunity provisions. Neither can be assessed without the text.

## What to watch

- The High Court ruling on the underlying case, reported as scheduled for 30 October 2026.
- Whether the Court of Appeal gives reasons for lifting the conservatory order.
- Publication of the definition of aggregated data and the immunity clauses.
- The Kenyan share of the financing in the budget.
